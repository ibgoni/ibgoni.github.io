Working Papers

Wired for Growth? Submarine Cables, Inland Infrastructure, and Local Development in Africa
October 2026
Abstract: Where does fast internet raise local economic activity? I study the 2009–2012 arrival of submarine fibre-optic cables in seventeen sub-Saharan African countries. Using a heterogeneity-robust difference-in-differences estimator, I compare locations within ten kilometres of the pre-existing terrestrial backbone with locations ten to twenty kilometres away in the same country, before and after cable arrival. Gridded GDP rises by 0.7 percent and night lights by 7.3 percent on average. Gains are larger near 3G or 4G towers and smaller farther from roads and the electricity transmission network, suggesting that inland last mile infrastructure helps determine who benefits from greater international bandwidth. To examine what these gains mean for households, I apply the same design to survey data. I also find suggestive evidence of increases in consumption and assets. The clearest change is a five-percentage-point increase in the share of working-age adults in non-agricultural work, with no detectable change in overall employment.
